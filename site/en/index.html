Nashir (ناشِر) for Chrome

Post to many Facebook groups, safely.

Nashir is a free, Arabic-first Chrome extension that posts to many Facebook groups for you. A built-in safety governor keeps it inside a call budget, with cooldowns and pacing. Everything stays on your device.

Download Nashir How to install

Nashir automates Facebook with your own session. That is against Facebook's Terms, and you use it at your own risk. Details

What it does

You write a post once and choose the groups. Nashir publishes it group by group at a safe pace, tells you what worked in each, and stops when Facebook asks for a check or signals a limit. It also helps you find the right groups and the right people, without leaving the extension.

Features

Campaigns

Write once, pick your groups, and see the result for every group.

Safety governor

A call budget, cooldowns, pacing and per-feature caps. A Facebook security check stops everything until you clear it.

Multiple accounts

Run campaigns from several of your own Facebook accounts.

Scheduling

Queue a campaign to start later instead of sitting at the screen.

Analytics

See what you posted, where, and how it went over time.

Audience extraction

Collect the people who reacted to or commented on a post, including phone numbers they wrote themselves in a comment, and export the list.

Lead Radar

Watch your groups for posts that look like someone wants to buy, and check them about every 15 minutes.

Auto first comment

Add your link or details as the first comment under a post, automatically.

Group discovery and joining

Find groups and join them in small batches: at most 8 a day, 3 per batch, with spacing between joins.

Facebook operations card

Facebook renames its internal identifiers from time to time. Nashir re-learns them from Facebook's own pages and shows which ones are current.

Arabic first

An Arabic interface by default, with English too, in light and dark themes.

Signed one-click updates

New versions come from GitHub Releases and are checked against a signature before anything is installed.

Install in four steps

Nashir is not on the Chrome Web Store, so you install it from GitHub. It takes a couple of minutes and needs no tools.

  1. Download and unzip

    Download nashir-<version>.zip from the latest release and unzip it into a normal folder such as Documents\Nashir. Do not use AppData, a temporary folder or the Downloads folder: Chrome reads the extension from this folder every time it starts, so it has to stay put.

    Download Nashir

  2. Turn on Developer mode

    Type chrome://extensions in Chrome's address bar and press Enter. Switch on Developer mode in the top corner of the page.

  3. Load the folder

    Click Load unpacked and choose the folder you unzipped. Nashir appears in the list. Pin it to the toolbar if you like.

  4. Link the folder once

    Open the Nashir dashboard. When it asks, choose the same folder again and allow access. This is what lets the extension update itself later, with one click.

How updates work

When a new version is released, a banner appears in the dashboard.

  1. Click Update in the banner.
  2. Allow access to the folder if Chrome asks.
  3. Nashir checks the release's signature first. Only if it matches does it write the new files, and then the extension reloads itself on the new version.

Prefer to do it by hand? Download the new zip, unzip it over the same folder, then open chrome://extensions and press Reload on Nashir.

Updates replace the extension's files only. Your campaigns and settings are kept by Chrome separately and stay as they are.

Safety and privacy

Built to go slowly

  • Every call to Facebook passes through one governor with a call budget, pacing and cooldowns. Recent counts show on the Logs page.
  • Group joining is capped at 8 a day and 3 per batch, with spacing between joins.
  • When Facebook signals a limit, Nashir pauses and shows the rule that matched.
  • When Facebook asks you to confirm it is you, Nashir stops all calls for that account until you finish the check.

Your data stays with you

  • Campaigns, groups, audiences and settings are stored in your browser, on your device.
  • There is no Nashir server and no telemetry. Nothing is collected about you or your activity.
  • Besides facebook.com, GitHub (update checks and downloads) is the only other address the extension contacts by itself. Links you click yourself, such as WhatsApp chat links, open normally.
  • The code is open and released under GPL-3.0, so you can read exactly what it does.

Use at your own risk. Nashir automates Facebook's internal endpoints using your own logged-in session. That is against Facebook's Terms and can lead to restrictions on your account. The safety governor lowers the risk. It cannot remove it.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if Facebook shows a security checkpoint?

Nashir recognises Facebook's "confirm it is you" check and stops every call for that account, because calls made during a block can make it last longer. It tells you to open Facebook and finish the check. After you do, you resume from the dashboard.

What is a cooldown?

When Facebook signals that you are going too fast, the governor pauses that kind of call for a while. The Logs page shows the rule that matched and how long the pause lasts. Waiting it out is the safe choice.

Why is Nashir not in the Chrome Web Store?

The Chrome Web Store is not a fit for this kind of automation, so Nashir is distributed from GitHub. Every release is signed, and the extension verifies the signature before it applies an update.

Why must the folder not be in AppData or a temporary folder?

Chrome loads the extension from that folder each time it starts, and one-click updates write new files into it. Temporary and Downloads folders get cleaned or moved, which would break the extension. A folder such as Documents\Nashir is stable and easy to find.

Will an update erase my data?

No. An update replaces the extension's files in the folder. Your data lives in Chrome's storage for the extension, not in that folder.

Is it really free?

Yes. Nashir is open source under GPL-3.0. There is no account to create and nothing to pay.

Can my Facebook account be restricted?

It can. Automating Facebook is against its Terms, whatever the pace. Nashir is built to be careful, but you use it at your own risk.
